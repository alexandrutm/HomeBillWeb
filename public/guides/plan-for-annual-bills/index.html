How to plan for annual bills in a monthly budget

A yearly bill can be easy to overlook when you review money one month at a time. Listing it early and translating it into a monthly planning figure can make the due date less surprising.

Published November 5, 2026 · 4 min read · By the HomeBill team

1. Make a list of bills that do not arrive monthly

Look back through the last year for payments such as home or vehicle insurance, annual memberships, registration fees, and service contracts. Note each bill's usual amount, approximate due date, and whether the amount tends to change. Check the provider's latest notice rather than relying on an old figure.

2. Convert the yearly amount into a planning amount

For a simple example, a €480 bill paid once a year is equivalent to €40 per month when spread evenly across 12 months (€480 ÷ 12). This is a way to understand the size of the upcoming cost, not a payment instruction or a guarantee that the next bill will be exactly €480.

Example: If a yearly policy was €480 last year, record the due month and use €40 as a monthly planning estimate. Confirm the renewal price before it is due.

3. Keep the due date and the monthly estimate distinct

The provider still expects payment on its actual due date. A monthly equivalent is useful for planning, but it does not mean the annual bill is paid in 12 instalments. If you choose to set money aside, use a method that suits your circumstances and verify the amount and timing yourself.

4. Review the list when amounts or dates change

Renewals can change in price, and some yearly payments may be cancelled or moved. Revisit your list when a renewal notice arrives and after each annual payment. For bills with variable costs, use the latest confirmed amount rather than treating last year's total as certain.
